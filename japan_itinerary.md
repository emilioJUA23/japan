## ❄️ Winter Timing Note

> With **Day 0 = 2028-01-07**, the calendar lines up like this:
> - **Tokyo D1–D7 = Jan 8–14** → the **January Grand Sumo Tournament** at Ryogoku Kokugikan (expected **Jan 9–23, 2028**; tournaments start on the 2nd Sunday of January, so confirm when the schedule is released). Sumo day = **D7**.
> - **D3 = Mon Jan 10 = Coming of Age Day**: kimono-clad 20-year-olds at Meiji Jingu, plus ceremonial archery.
> - **Hakuba D9–D12 = Jan 16–19** → mid-January is prime powder season, before the Chinese New Year crowds.
> - **D27 = Feb 3 = Setsubun**: bean-throwing festivals at Tokyo temples.
>
> ⚠️ **Chinese New Year falls on Jan 26, 2028** (Kyoto D19). Expect busier Kyoto temples and higher hotel prices that week, so **book Kyoto early**.

> **Winter realities:** sunset is ~16:45 in Tokyo in mid-January (~17:20 in Kyoto by early February). Many temples and gardens close at 16:00–17:00. Cities run 0–10°C; Hakuba runs −10 to 0°C.
>
> **Alternative:** Start Feb 1 instead to skip Chinese New Year entirely. You lose sumo (there's no February tournament) but still get peak snow.

## 🎫 Transport Strategy: No JR Pass This Time

> - **There is no 30-day JR Pass.** Only 7/14/21-day passes exist (~¥50,000 / ¥80,000 / ¥100,000).
> - This route is a straight line, so **point-to-point Shinkansen tickets total ≈ ¥65,000–75,000** (~$440–500), less than a 21-day pass. The Hokuriku Arch Pass (¥35,000 / 7 days) doesn't pay off with this pacing either.
> - Buy Shinkansen tickets at station machines or JR offices (or the JR East / smartEX apps). Use **Suica** for every local train, subway and bus.
> - Without a JR Pass, the **Nozomi** trains are fine. They're the fastest option on Tokyo–Osaka–Hiroshima.
> - 💡 **Luggage forwarding (takkyubin):** on D7, send your big suitcase from the Tokyo hotel straight to your Hakuba lodging (~¥2,500–3,500, arrives in 1–2 days). You travel to Nagano and Shibu Onsen with just a daypack.

## 🗼 DAY 1 — TOKYO: Arrival + First Steps

**📍 City:** Tokyo | **🏨 Hotel:** Tokyo Week 1 (Shinjuku, Asakusa, or Ueno area)
**✈️ Arrive:** NRT (Narita) or HND (Haneda)

### At the Airport (allow 1.5–2h)
1. **Immigration:** Present passport. Japan arrival card filled on the plane. If registered on Visit Japan Web, use e-gate (faster).
2. **Customs:** Declare any cash over ¥1 million. Straightforward otherwise.
3. **Get Suica IC card:** At the JR ticket machines (or 7-Eleven in the terminal). Load ¥5,000–¥10,000.
4. **Pocket WiFi / SIM pickup:** If you pre-reserved, counter is in arrivals hall (IIJmio, Sakura Mobile, JAL ABC).
5. **ATM:** 7-Eleven ATMs in the airport accept foreign cards — withdraw ¥20,000–¥30,000 cash.

### Airport → Hotel
| Option                      | Route                           | Time        | Cost                     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------ |
| **Narita Express (N'EX)** ✅ | NRT → Shinjuku/Shibuya (direct) | ~80 min     | ~¥3,250 (~$22)           |
| Limousine Bus               | NRT → major Tokyo hotels        | ~90–120 min | ¥3,200 (~$21)            |
| Taxi                        | NRT → central Tokyo             | ~75–90 min  | ¥20,000+ (~$130) — avoid |

> **From HND (Haneda):** Tokyo Monorail → Hamamatsucho → JR, or Keikyu Line. Both work with Suica (~¥500–700).
> HND is 30–40 min closer to the city than NRT.

### Afternoon / Evening — Gentle First Day
- **Rest at hotel:** drop bags, shower, decompress. Jet lag from Guatemala (UTC-6) to Tokyo (UTC+9) is a 15-hour difference — your body thinks it's 2am.
- **Explore your neighborhood on foot:** find the nearest konbini (convenience store). Buy onigiri, melon pan, a Pocari Sweat — your introduction to Japanese convenience store food. It's genuinely excellent.
- **Dinner nearby:** a simple bowl of ramen or tonkatsu near the hotel. Don't overthink it — this is a rest day.
- **Sleep by 9–10pm Tokyo time** to reset your internal clock. Resist the temptation to stay up.

### Transport Summary
- N'EX from NRT to hotel area: ~¥3,250 (~$22). There's no JR Pass on this trip (see Transport Strategy above)
- Suica for any short metro hops: ~¥200 per ride

### Afternoon — Setsubun (February 3) 👹
> **Setsubun** marks the traditional start of spring. Temples hold **mamemaki**: roasted soybeans are thrown to drive out demons (*"Oni wa soto! Fuku wa uchi!"*). At big Tokyo temples, **celebrities and sumo wrestlers** throw beans from a stage into the crowd.
